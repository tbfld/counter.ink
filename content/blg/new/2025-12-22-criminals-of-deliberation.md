It just kinda clicked that the worst thing about MacAskill’s book *What We Owe the Future* might be that the basic insight is right but that he inverts the argument’s plain-as-day implication: that “criminals of deliberation” — those who are demonstrably committed to criminality, as distinct from those who merely happen to commit crimes of passion — should be extirpated. To be specific: ecocidaires, oligarchs, public and private actors engaged in large-scale corruption, etc. If you want to argue about what constitutes criminality, be my guest, or rather be someone else’s guest — because that debate’s mostly hot air, imo. It’s self-evident that our collective goal as a species should be to allow everyone to live fully and freely. When what exactly that means to different people comes into conflict, as it will, then mediate and adjudicate those tensions on a class basis in every sense of the term, i.e., a model that seeks to balance qualitative (those less able) and quantitative (as many as possible) optimizations. In this view, those who deliberately try to abuse and game social, political, economic, and technical systems in order to exploit or harm others on a stochastic basis are, by definition, the worst — and should be treated accordingly. IOW, those who would deny others’ civil and human rights at scale need to be punished “at scale.” (Put plainly: How do you deter a trillionaire and his minions?) Every single word of this can and should be debated, but at least these would be better debates than the ones we’re currently having. All of which makes me a sort of mutant post-Maoist / cultural leftist, I think. And, I mean, a dilettante and poseur, of course, but aren’t we all? Maybe my biz card should read SYSTEMS-ORIENTED DILETTANTE.
