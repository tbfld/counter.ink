You know how most street signs are mounted on “U-channel’ steel posts? The ones with holes drilled out every few inches? That design balances flexibility (it’s easy to cut and allows for different sign sizes and combinations), cost (it’s cheap), structural strength (it doesn’t bend in the face if weather or casual abuse), and safety (but it *breaks* easily). That’s all part of a framework called MASH-compliance, for the [Manual for Assessing Safety Hardware]([file:///Users/tbyfield/Desktop/MASH-2_TableOfContents.pdf](https://store.transportation.org/Common/DownloadContentFiles?id=1539)) — which covers guardrails, work zone barriers, hi-viz colors, and so on. Specifications for “breakaway” signage bases are one of MASH’s fundamental principles. This is incredibly important life-saving stuff — it softens impact, minimizes knock-on effects, reduces gas-tank punctures, and so on. It’s also incredibly *mandatory* stuff: federal and state DOTs, OSHA, unions, insurers and reinsurers, everyone loves it because it’s part of the technical regime on which the everyday life we take for granted is founded. I knew ask that because I’m a technical standards nerd, but I didn’t know this: Flock, that creepy surveillance company, is too busy making money to follow the rules — so they’re putting up their BS with the same level of artistry and care as those electric scooter companies.
