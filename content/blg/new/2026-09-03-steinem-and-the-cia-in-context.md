Steinem’s corpse is barely cold and right on cue, here come the copy-paste wing of the left pissing on her because she (like many rising leftish figures of the era) had some dealings with the CIA etc. What’s missing? Another wing of the left who are willing to *defend* that, and not just with half-mumbled mealy-mouthed excuses. What, *defend the indefensible*, you ask? Yes, because the period in question, 1958–1962, was a *very* different time. US intel services still enjoyed a broadly positive rep for their role in defeating European fascism, and the cultural sphere was awash in people who’d worked either for or with the OSS. Little was publicly known about CIA abuses in (for ex) Iran or Guatemala, and Vietnam hadn’t broadly discredited US military/diplomatic approaches abroad yet. But that’s all just context; the heart of the issue was Steinem’s own questing drive to push boundaries and open new spaces. Feminism wasn’t exactly an established field or form yet: its alliances, its boundaries, its relations to other movements and intellectual models were far from defined. So YES, we should expect that a figure like Steinem would be have been forward and hungry to engage with all kinds of organizations and movements in pursuit of her vision. We need more of that spirit now, and less copy-paste wing historical revisionism.
