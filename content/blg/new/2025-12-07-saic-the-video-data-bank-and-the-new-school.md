Re [An Open Letter to SAIC Regarding Video Data Bank](https://filmmakermagazine.com/132507-an-open-letter-to-saic-regarding-video-data-bank/)

If we were in Europe, someone would have connected the School of the Art Institute of Chicago shuttering the Video Data Bank with the catastrophe at the New School — because most of TNS is actually a design/art school, Parsons. What’s the connection? Where even to begin? Ever since the Reagan/Thatcher period, governments at every level have been shifting their burdens onto educational institutions — OT1H defunding them, OT0H imposing growing regimes of regulations and impositions on them — passing much of US higher ed really to the brink. But higher ed isn’t monolithic, it’s a tangle of cultural distinctions, within which art and design schools have a strange place: ‘impractical’ schools whose vocational pasts have become associated with luxury futures. Their institutional finances are less in lockstep with the rest of higher ed than with woolly stuff like the art market, the relative prestige of design, the accessibility of new technologies, the vagaries of expanding trade relations, and so on. Trump’s attacks on higher ed are, in large part, the culmination of that Reaganite/Thatcherite program. These schools are basically totem poles exemplifying the conservative imaginary of higher ed’s contradictions — so it’s no accident if they start to fail as a sector. I suspect that’s in the works, but then again I’ve been warning about this for nearly twenty years. Anyway, we’re in the US, so no one else will make that connection — you’ll have to settle for me. 😹
