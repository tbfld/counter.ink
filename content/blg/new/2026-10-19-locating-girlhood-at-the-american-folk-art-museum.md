Over the weekend I saw one of the best museum exhibitions I’ve seen in decades at the [American Folk Art Museum](https://folkartmuseum.org/), surprisingly enough — “[Locating Girlhood: Place and Identity in Early American ‘Schoolgirl’ Art](https://folkartmuseum.org/exhibitions/locating-girlhood/).” Run don’t walk, then catch your breath and compose yourself before you go in. The museum itself is a treat, from the get-go: *Do I need to pay? Nah. Do you need to look in my bag? Nah.* That exchange with the guard at the front in itself was like a micro-dose of time travel. Exhibitions like this — with a naughties-style academicky book title, purporting to surface some lost form of cultural complexity, and so on — are a dime a dozen, and few succeed, imo. But the works in the exhibition are astonishing, wondrous, restorative, and, if you give your time and attention to them, transporting. Piece by piece, it felt like the show conjured to a very rare degree the intimate subjectivity — the quiet, the piety, the sense of a time that both stretches forth and whose end looms, the genealogies, the light, the spaces  — of the young women who made these works. Through the end of Feb 2027, but *really* don’t put it off.
