This NYT article on Back-as-Satoshi is drunk and needs to go home. The first half of it is jaw-dropping coincidences like *both Back and Satoshi* were interested in public-key cryptography and distributed computing — yes, and everyone at a Pez convention is interested in dispensers. When you look closer you find gems like this, where he presents as evidence that they casually expressed the idea that they’re better at coding than rhetoric TWELVE YEARS APART!!! I mean, if that isn’t a smoking gun, I don’t know what is. And that isn’t isolated. He follows it up with an argument that Satoshi must have been British because he used “bloody” once in 2010 and Adam had used it in 1998 then argued TWENTY-FIVE YEARS LATER that he had never used that word — 🧐 “Why deny using a word he had in fact used so adamantly if he had nothing to hide?” 🤨 And don’t even get me going on the stylomety arguments, which FFS *I* contributed to in the mid-’90s. The only compelling argument involves the coming and going of Satoshi and Back, but even so: “For instance, Mr. Back didn’t have a good answer \[16 years later] for why he disappeared from the Cryptography list during the period Satoshi was active, other than to say he was busy with work.” OK, now if THAT isn’t a smoking gun, I don’t know what is!!! 😂 The author could be right, maybe Back is Satoshi, but this article is just shameful. And indicative of just how willing the NYT is to give front-page space to people who are comprehensively ignorant of the subjects they were it about.
