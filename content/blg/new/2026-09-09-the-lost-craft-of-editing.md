[This](https://www.theguardian.com/tv-and-radio/2026/sep/07/seminal-series-ends-six-decades-70-up) is a really nice piece, and it could and should be so much better with some attentive editing. Phrases like “coming to its final conclusion” and “ahead of her time in challenging conventional norms” are the tell, but that’s just petty stuff. The bigger picture is that editing — not the commissioning and acquiring side but the attentive craft of helping to shape what writers say — was the hidden key to a particular communitarian model of writing. The disappearance of that role (or, as more would say now, *function*) and the loss of that craft have accelerated the transformation of writing into something more like a commodity. That’s not necessarily bad; it’s a flip side of the opening of creative fields to many more people, the demise of a certain kind of (as they say now) gatekeeping, and another stage in the flowering of new forms and new audiences. — all absolutely necessary. So, no, this isn’t a paean to troubling types like [Jason Epstein](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jason_Epstein) — though it kinda is one for heroes like [Barney Rosset](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barney_Rosset) of Grove, [John Martin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Martin_(publisher)) of Black Sparrow, [Robert Silvers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robert_B._Silvers) of NYRB, and [Juan Garcia de Oteyza](https://aperture.org/editorial/juan-garcia-de-oteyza-1962-2013/) of Eridanos (all men, I know — see below). But if you asked writers whether they’d rather have a slower, less crowded, and more forgiving world with more consistently committed institutional supporters or a breakneck landscape of ever-increasing precarity, I’m pretty sure you know how they’d answer. And I expect that many readers would agree as well.

*Addendum:* It’s no secret that publishing was a man’s man’s man’s world. The editors I most enjoyed working with were *all* women — Sara Bershtel at Pantheon, Altie Karper at Schocken, Ramona Nadaff at Zone, Dawn Davis at the New Press. And then there are some serious heroes like Toni Morrison. None of them cultivated a heroic reputation.
