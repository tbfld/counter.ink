Re: [Renee Good memorial site in south Minneapolis doused in gasoline](https://www.startribune.com/renee-good-memorial-site-doused-in-gasoline/601584109)

This is plainly a hate crime, but in the US it could never be prosecuted as such because 🤔 for some strange reason 🤔 we define hate crimes in terms of categories or classes: race, religion, color, national origin, and occasionally sex (not quite gender yet), orientation, or disabilities. Hate crimes can, of course, extend beyond membership in a class — say, the white civil rights activists murdered in the South in the 1960s were clearly victims of hate crimes. It doesn’t take much of an imaginative leap to see that Renee Good and Alex Pretti were victims of hate crimes as well, or by extension that the desecration of a memorial to Good is likewise a hate crime. It seems to me that the challenge before us won’t be to restore what Trump & Co have broken — it’s the much more ambitious task of reimagining *all* of this in even better ways. I don’t know how to do that, but I do know it’s possible. I’m pretty sure it would require much more expansive, flexible thinking than most of what passes for leftism in the US these days. That in itself is a problem, and it’s the main reason I give the left so much grief.
