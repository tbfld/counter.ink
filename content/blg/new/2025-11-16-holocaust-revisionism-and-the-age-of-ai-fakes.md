Not so long ago, the specter of Holocaust revisionism was seen as so severe that the institutional response was an ethos of *absolute* caution. That’s why, for example, the US Holocaust Memorial Museum in DC made a point of explicitly noting facsimile objects in all its exhibition — to fend off any accusations that the account it presented was false. But that’s just one example. The Hamburg Institute for Social Research — which played in immense role in dispelling the myth that the Holocaust was perpetrated by “special” military units, and that the Wehrmacht (basically, the regular army) were innocent — assembled a photographic exhibition documenting their analysis in the mid-’90s. It was set to tour the US but was canceled because the insignia in a handful of photos were found to have been misinterpreted. I remember this well, because I worked on the book for the New Press. There are many more examples that demonstrate just how sharp those sensitivities were. However you feel about that or the larger context, it’s surprising — in a pretty ancient Greek tragedy way — to see the historical coincidence of the rise of AI slop and, in a very different context and for very different reasons, the dissolution of Israel’s claim to exceptional status stemming from the Holocaust. This image drives that home. Soon enough, AI slop will be indistinguishable from actual photos for everyone but those with direct pre-AI experience with the ~originals. And with that, though by no means solely because of that, the past will mutate into something we’ve never quite seen before.
