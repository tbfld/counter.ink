Today’s Imma-up-this goes out to AH and AK, in response to [the AI bot on the AoIR list](https://lists.aoir.org/archives/list/air-l@lists.aoir.org/thread/KAO7FUOOJGSZN4XAM7BSDDDWQKAAQREU/):

> I’m quickly coming to the conclusion that critiques of AI originating from the ~humanities are intellectually bankrupt, for the most part — basically reactionary special pleading “on stilts,” as the saying goes. That’s not to say they’re substantively wrong or morally misdirected; it is to say that a stopped clock (or maybe *slopped* clock) can be right twice a day. I just don’t believe that they’re deeply rooted in principle rather than in, say, fear, frustration, or ressentiment. More specifically, while I sympathize with people’s frustration at reply-all meltdowns (though was it actually one of those? seems unlikely), it kinda boggles the mind that INTERNET RESEARCHERS would react to such a novel phenomenon — and, at least on its face, surprisingly candid — with histrionic pearl-clutching, tantrums, “fandom flounces,” and misc internet drama nonsense. And I don’t say that from some airy-fairy abstraction, I moderated nettime for 25 F-NG YEARS, which is the equivalent of having survived the Somme, Verdun, Passchendaele, Gallipoli, *and* Ypres. 😹 And nor are these issues in past. When the wave of AI bots requesting accounts on our mastodon instance became a tsunami and the other mods started to lose it, I suggested simply de-automating the process and actually, you know, talking with people — which turned out to be win-win-win. AoIR is a different kettle of fish, because nettime was, from the beginning, reflexively and critically concerned with the power relations of open/closed/moderated/etc. But imagine for a moment if the list had been able to say “hold off on your reactions, and let’s try to have a civil chat with this thing to see what good might come of it,” that would have been *so* much more interesting. And more in keeping with what we’ll all need to learn to do, like it or not.
