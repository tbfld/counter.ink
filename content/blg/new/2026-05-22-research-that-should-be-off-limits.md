Are there any comprehensive principles for distinguishing and defining ideas and subjects that should not, under any circumstances, be researched? I started to ask a question like that in the early ’90s, when I came close to studying forensic anthropology. My interest at the time was driven by having worked on too many books about US mil/intel abuses. It seemed to me then (it still does) that — just for example — techniques for exterminating everyone in a village or even a small town should just be off-limits, period. It was later in that decade that I started to think in less exceptional terms and more systematically — or, more specifically, *about* potentially “systematic” structures, like the domain name system. It’s only in the last decade or so that I’ve started to connect those two threads and wonder how we can define the idea of fields and practices that are absolutely off-limits in every respect. As our destructive capacity grows by orders of magnitude — say, shadow libraries + gain-of-function virology + AI + CRISPR + PCR = DIY biowarfare — we’ll need to start asking questions like this very urgently *and answering them very forcefully*. If we don’t, we have maybe one generation left if we’re lucky. So what does this have to do with preschool teachers wearing bodycams to “develop AI models”? I don’t know, but I think it’s a fine example of the kinds of questions we need to ask. If your reaction is “wtf, that doesn’t make any sense! they’re totally unrelated”, my reply is this: I’m more interested in the survival of our world than in propping up disciplinary boundaries — which in any case were mostly administrative lifehacks for a kind of institution that’s dying before our very eyes.
