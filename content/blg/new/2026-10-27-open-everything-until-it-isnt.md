I’ve been riding this hobbyhorse for a decade at least — and what we we call “AI” now only accelerates it. We love “open” everything until we don’t and start talking about privacy or safeguards or whatever, but those limits are ad hoc and woolly af. The main factor that made nuclear nonproliferation was the sheer *cost* — only nation states could afford it. That’s not true of ‘desktop’ genetic engineering, whose costs are somewhere between Breaking Bad and prosumer video. Everyone loves inherited notions of human and civil rights, but a *lot* of people would change their tune but fast if they learned, say, that Musk or Thiel was privately funding bioweapon research. (If I were a betting man, I’d lay good money on a bet that at least one of them *is doing it now*.) So back to one of my fave rants: certain kinds of punishment need to keep pace with inflation in order to deter, for example, the buying power of *trillionaires*. So here’s a thought experiment: what threatened punishment would be enough to deter even Musk, who can buy armies of proxies and cutouts to protect himself? Hint: (1) it needs to be strictly limited to crimes of deliberation, and (2) it’ll need to be *extremely* cruel and unusual. But here’s the thing: A few years ago this was a tight experiment — now it’s just basic diligence.
